# Agriculture

**Summary**: Notes on agricultural mapping, field boundary delineation, crop monitoring, and the models and datasets behind them.
**Last updated**: 2026-10-03

- [Google Agricultural Understanding — ALU and AMED](https://agri.withgoogle.com/): Two LinkedIn posts by Saurav (11 and 13 Sep 2026) on Google's field-level agriculture platform. *Agricultural Landscape Understanding* (ALU) turns imagery into field boundaries, acreage, vegetation, water bodies and landscape structure. *Agricultural Monitoring and Event Detection* (AMED) adds historical and in-season crop monitoring per field: crop seasons and labels. ALU is visible in Google Earth, a BigQuery dataset is promised, API access is by application, and coverage is mainly India (Telangana), expanding across the Global South. The platform's line: *"Fields are the atomic units of agriculture."* Saurav's [first post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/saurav-geoai_geoai-googleai-geospatial-share-7504235550267064320-QfKs) argues the value is in enrichment: *"what becomes possible when every farm is represented as a structured spatial entity that can continuously be enriched with satellite, weather, crop and business data."* His [second](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/saurav-geoai_geoai-googleai-remotesensing-ugcPost-7504808157567717376-Xwjb) introduces AMED. *Keywords: Google ALU, AMED, field boundaries, India, crop monitoring, agri-lending*
  - See [[LinkedIn]]. Compare open field-boundary efforts: Fields of the World and Trazo on this page. Related: [[Embeddings]], [[Geospatial_Platforms]]

- [Mapping agricultural dynamics in displacement-affected landscapes using satellite foundation model embeddings](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aeabbc): LinkedIn post by Catherine Nakalembe, 1 October 2026: *"Maps and data alone do not equal better policy—it's what we do with them that matters."* The paper (Asare-Ansah, Van Den Hoek, Adebayo, Song & Nakalembe, *Environmental Research Letters* 21, DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/aeabbc, open access) uses 10 m AlphaEarth embeddings to build annual cropland maps, with sample-based area estimates, for northern Uganda's refugee-hosting districts from 2017 to 2024. Cropland grew fast until 2019, dipped in 2020 (COVID and floods), peaked at about 447,800 ha in 2022, then halved to about 223,000 ha by 2024 as aid cuts and drought hit. Farming clusters near settlements and tracks rainfall anomalies. Nakalembe's own write-up is a [LinkedIn article](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/using-googles-satellite-embeddings-data-uncover-rapid-catherine-gl71c). *Keywords: refugee settlements, Uganda, cropland change, AlphaEarth, area estimation, food security*
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/catherinenakalembe_maps-and-data-alone-do-not-equal-better-policyit-ugcPost-7511450384788459521-NkFi); see [[LinkedIn]].

- [Towards accessible smallholder crop classification in the groundnut basin of Senegal](https://doi.org/10.1088/2976-601X/aea0b8): LinkedIn post by Madeline Lisaius, 29 September 2026, on the last paper of her PhD (Lisaius, Keshav, Blake & Atzberger, *Environmental Research: Food Systems*, CC BY 4.0). It proposes judging crop-type mapping on three axes, performance, temporal transferability and accessibility, and tests TESSERA and AlphaEarth embeddings against traditional baselines. TESSERA did best: it needed 78% less compute than a time-series approach, less custom feature engineering, and was about 28% more accurate (about +0.10 weighted F1) in one temporal-transfer test. Cambridge's [news story](https://www.cam.ac.uk/research/news/ai-tool-for-mapping-smallholder-crops-proves-itself-in-senegal) reports 84% accuracy. *Keywords: smallholder agriculture, Senegal, crop classification, TESSERA, AlphaEarth, accessibility*
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/madeline-lisaius_excited-to-share-the-release-of-the-final-share-7510601812064858112-UyiA); see [[LinkedIn]].

- [Monitoring the World's Grasslands and Livestock](https://landcarbonlab.org/events/grasslands-a-complete-view-of-overlooked-land/): A Land & Carbon Lab webinar (2 September 2026) marking the completion of the Global Pasture Watch consortium's first suite of open global grassland datasets, timed with the UN International Year of Rangelands & Pastoralists. - [Trazo — field boundaries for South America](https://wri.github.io/trazo/landing/): LinkedIn post by Land & Carbon Lab, 14 July 2026: *"The field level is the starting point for every sustainability decision in agriculture, from tracing a crop through the supply chain to monitoring how land is used over time."* Land & Carbon Lab and Arizona State University released Trazo, an open-source suite of AI models that segment crop field boundaries from Sentinel-2 across South America. It extends the Fields of the World benchmark with training data from 17 ecoregions, including the Chaco, Amazon, Pampas and Chiquitania, and adds 40,000+ boundary geometries. Soy fields reach 77% pixel IoU, rising to 84% with fine-tuned models. The model and full training data are public. Background is in the blog post [Why Accurately Mapping Crop Field Boundaries is Essential for Sustainable Supply Chains](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/mapping-crop-field-boundaries/) (Grupp, Kerner, Tárano, Tan; 2 July 2026) and a [WRI technical note](https://www.wri.org/research/field-boundaries-south-america-technical-note). Code: [wri/trazo](https://github.com/wri/trazo), CC-BY-4.0. *Keywords: field boundaries, South America, soy, Fields of the World, Sentinel-2, supply chains*
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/the-field-level-is-the-starting-point-ugcPost-7482825223092846592-ImjM); see [[LinkedIn]]. The bit.ly links were resolved and their `utm_*` tracking stripped.
  - Builds directly on Fields of the World, further down this page. Its deforestation counterpart, Land & Carbon Lab's comparison of soy deforestation datasets, is on [[Forestry]].

- [AmirmasoudCS/Crop-Yield-Prediction](https://github.com/AmirmasoudCS/Crop-Yield-Prediction): *Machine learning project investigating whether crop-specific linear regression models can outperform a single global model for crop yield prediction using historical agricultural and environmental data.* A deliberately simple study asking one question: does fitting a separate linear regression per crop beat fitting one model across all crops? On the Kaggle Crop Yield Prediction dataset — 28,242 observations, 101 areas, 10 crop types, 1990–2013, with rainfall, pesticide use and average temperature as features — the crop-specific models reach R² 0.8966 and MAE 15,879.76 against the global baseline's R² 0.6424 and MAE 31,273.75, roughly 49% lower error. The repo walks the full workflow: EDA, preparation, train/validation/test split, global baseline, per-crop models, evaluation, comparison, then coefficient interpretation. Jupyter, MIT. *Keywords: crop yield prediction, linear regression, per-crop models, Kaggle dataset, baseline comparison, coefficient analysis*
  - Worth keeping for the framing rather than the accuracy: it is the cheapest possible version of the specialization argument that runs through [[Foundation_Models]] — does a model per domain beat one model for everything — answered here with linear regression on tabular data instead of pretrained backbones.
  - Treat the numbers with care: yields differ by orders of magnitude between crops, so splitting by crop removes much of the variance a single global model has to absorb, and the gain is partly definitional.

- [Mid-season parcel-based crop type mapping using multi-source time series of big Earth observations and deep learning](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hazhir-bahrami-ab7723104_github-tenor-inrscropmappingproject-share-7497636823557550080-JnDZ/): LinkedIn publication announcement by lead author Hazhir Bahrami. The framing is that timing is the point, not just accuracy — *"what matters most is having crop-type maps during the growing season (not after it ends) when they can actually inform decisions."* Six models were trained on multi-temporal Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 over Quebec for 2020–2024 — four deep learning (Transformers, hybrid LSTM–Transformers, 1D CNN, LSTM) against Random Forest and XGBoost baselines — mapping corn, soybean, wheat, hay and others, with field boundaries and crop labels from La Financière Agricole du Québec (FADQ) as reference. The Transformer won at 0.97 overall accuracy on validation and 0.93 on test. Two claims the author calls out: applied to entirely unseen 2025 data the models produced mid-season maps at ~92% overall accuracy *"with no additional in-year training data"*, which he positions against *"one of the field's biggest open challenges: uncertainty around future inference"*; and Google's AlphaEarth embeddings fed to plain RF/XGB matched the deep learning models on Sentinel data — with the caveat, stated in the post, that AlphaEarth is an annual product included to gauge embedding quality *"rather than as a direct mid-season equivalent."* Published in *Big Earth Data*, 16 August 2026, Bahrami, Homayouni, Champagne, McNairn, Daneshfar, Chokmani, Nteziyaremye and Zannou, DOI [10.1080/20964471.2026.2705616](https://doi.org/10.1080/20964471.2026.2705616). Code and supporting data: [TENOR-INRS/CropMapping_Project](https://github.com/TENOR-INRS/CropMapping_Project). *Keywords: mid-season crop mapping, Transformer, Sentinel-1/2, AlphaEarth embeddings, unseen year transfer, Quebec FADQ*
  - The annual-vs-mid-season caveat is the interesting tension: an annual embedding product matching per-season deep learning on accuracy does not make it usable for in-season decisions, which is the whole premise of the paper. Related argument on [[Foundation_Models]].
  - Both shortlinks in the post were resolved before filing: the data availability link points at the same GitHub repository as the code link. Tracking parameters were stripped from the post URL.
  - Related: [[Deep_Learning]], [[Remote_Sensing]], [[Foundation_Models]], [[Embeddings]], [[LinkedIn]], [[Code_Repositories]]

- [Fields of the World inference app](https://fieldsofthe.world/ftw-inference-app/): Fields of the world inference website. FTW is "an open ecosystem for agricultural field boundary detection" spanning a global dataset, models, inference tooling and web apps, all built on open Sentinel-2. The benchmark holds over 1.6 million labeled parcels across 24 countries on four continents paired with multi-temporal Sentinel-2; the 2024-25 global inference product covers 3.17 billion field polygons across 241 countries at 10 m with confidence scores and F1 between 0.74 and 0.89 across tested countries. The Explorer app browses those boundaries on an interactive map, downloads polygons for a region, and can run inference in the browser with no setup. Sponsored by Taylor Geospatial and co-developed with Microsoft's AI for Good Lab, with ASU's Kerner Lab, WashU and Clark University, hosted via Source Cooperative and Wherobots. *Keywords: FTW, field boundaries, 3.17 billion polygons, Sentinel-2, browser inference, Taylor Geospatial*
  - **User Advisory Group** ([Taylor Geospatial, 3 Aug 2026](https://taylorgeospatial.org/news/introducing-ftw-user-advisory-group/)): FTW recruited 10–15 advisors from government, UN, NGO, academic and commercial organisations for a 12-month term (four virtual sessions) to test tools and give structured feedback. Applications closed on 14 August 2026. The post describes FTW as global 10 m Sentinel-2 field boundaries built by ASU, WashU, Clark, Wherobots and Microsoft AI for Good.

- [AgriFM: A Multi-source Temporal Remote Sensing Foundation Model for Crop Mapping](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.21357): AgriFM: A Multi-source Temporal Remote Sensing Foundation Model for Crop Mapping. Li, Liang, Chen and colleagues, arXiv 2505.21357, submitted 27 May 2025 (v3 January 2026). A Transformer foundation model for crop mapping built on a modified Video Swin Transformer backbone doing hierarchical spatiotemporal feature extraction, trained on 25M+ global image samples fusing MODIS, Landsat-8/9 and Sentinel-2 time series. Reports gains over both task-specific deep learning models and general-purpose remote sensing foundation models on agricultural downstream tasks. *Keywords: foundation model, crop mapping, video swin transformer, multi-source fusion, MODIS Landsat Sentinel-2, spatiotemporal*

- [Lacuna Fund agricultural datasets](https://lacunafund.org/datasets/agriculture/): Lacuna Fund agricultural datasets. The Lacuna Fund, co-founded in 2020 by The Rockefeller Foundation, Google.org and Canada's IDRC, funds equitably labeled datasets for machine learning in low- and middle-income contexts. Its agriculture portfolio covers sub-Saharan Africa, both earth-observation-referenced and other parts of the crop and animal system; the first round put $1.1M into six African projects. Examples include image and spectrometry data for five food security crops (cassava, maize, beans, bananas, cocoa) and an open crop-type classification dataset spanning Kenya, Mali, Togo, Rwanda, Uganda, Ethiopia, Malawi, Zambia, Tanzania, Namibia, Sudan and Nigeria. *Keywords: labeled datasets, sub-Saharan Africa, food security crops, crop type classification, open data, ML for development*

- [Côte d'Ivoire Byte-Sized Agriculture Challenge](https://zindi.africa/competitions/cote-divoire-byte-sized-agriculture-challenge/data): Zindi Agricultural Challeneges. A Zindi competition to classify cocoa, oil palm and rubber plantations from multi-temporal Sentinel-2 imagery, using one year of seasonal signal. - [Time2Agri: Temporal Pretext Tasks for Agricultural Monitoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04366): Time2Agri: Temporal Pretext Tasks for Agricultural Monitoring. Gupta & Sobti, arXiv 2507.04366, submitted 6 July 2025. Proposes three agriculture-specific self-supervised pretext tasks — Time-Difference Prediction, Temporal Frequency Prediction, and Future-Frame Prediction — that exploit the seasonal cycles of agricultural landscapes rather than generic image pretext tasks. On the SICKLE and FTW India datasets it reports 69.6% IoU for crop mapping and 30.7% MAPE for yield prediction, beating existing remote sensing foundation models. *Keywords: self-supervised learning, pretext tasks, seasonality, yield prediction, SICKLE, crop mapping*

- [From General to Specialized: The Need for Foundational Models in Agriculture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05390): From General to Specialized: The Need for Foundational Models in Agriculture. Nedungadi, Xiong, Potze, Van Bree, Lin, Rußwurm & Athanasiadis, arXiv 2507.05390, submitted 7 July 2025. A position paper arguing that although general-purpose foundation models have taken hold in remote sensing and climate science, "their application in challenges related to agriculture...remains under-explored", and that agriculture needs its own model — which they call CropFM — designed for crop mapping, phenology estimation and yield prediction rather than adapted from general ones. *Keywords: CropFM, position paper, domain-specific foundation models, phenology, yield prediction, agriculture AI*

- [agribound](https://github.com/montimaj/agribound): Python package for delineating field boundaries. A toolkit by Sangram Majumdar that combines seven approaches to agricultural field boundary delineation — YOLO object detection, semantic segmentation (FTW, DINOv3, Prithvi), Mask R-CNN instance segmentation, embedding-based clustering, and multi-model ensembling. Works with Landsat, Sentinel-2, HLS, NAIP, SPOT and pre-computed embeddings (AlphaEarth, TESSERA) via Google Earth Engine or local GeoTIFFs, filters non-agricultural polygons using NLCD / Dynamic World / C3S land cover, and exports fiboa-compliant vectors. Apache 2.0. *Keywords: field boundaries, crop mapping, segmentation, Google Earth Engine, fiboa, python package*
  - **Update, v1.0.1** ([LinkedIn, Sayantan "Monty" Majumdar, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sayantanmajumdar_remotesensing-gis-agriculture-ugcPost-7511112629743132672-jrS-)): Majumdar presented *"Geospatial AI for Agricultural Field Boundary Delineation: Challenges and Opportunities"* to the NASA EarthRISE/UAH Geo-AI working group. The toolkit now runs one config across Delineate Anything v2, Fields of The World, fine-tuned Mask R-CNN and DINOv3, Prithvi-EO-2.0, label-free clustering of Google Satellite Embedding and TESSERA, and optional SAM 2 refinement. Imagery can be Landsat, Sentinel-2, HLS, NAIP, SPOT 6/7 or your own GeoTIFFs. His finding: *"the barriers we measured to administrative use of AI field boundaries are not the model architecture. They are resolution, reporting practice, and the filters applied after the model."* The SAM 3 backends and the Claude-driven agent are still untested. See [[LinkedIn]].
