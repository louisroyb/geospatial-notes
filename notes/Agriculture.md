- [Detecting organic vs conventional farming from Sentinel-2 time series](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24552): LinkedIn post by Jan Hemmerling, 26 March 2026, on the preprint by Hemmerling, Schwieder, Rufin, Thomas, Tulbure, Hostert & Erasmi. A TSViT vision transformer on intra-annual Sentinel-2 time series over Bavaria reaches up to 0.94 overall accuracy. Organic winter rye, wheat and oat are separable (F1 ≥ 0.8), but grassland, orchards, grapevine and hops are not (≤ 0.4). Wider spatial context helps; multitask learning with crop type helps only slightly. *"Phenology is likely a strong contributor, but the extent to which specific management practices play a role ... remains an open question."* *Keywords: organic farming, management practices, TSViT, Sentinel-2, Bavaria, crop type*

- [Survey Protocol Cards for Crop Maps](https://doi.org/10.31223/X5WR1F): LinkedIn post by Akram Zaytar (Microsoft AI for Good), 4 April 2026, on two ICLR 2026 ML4RS workshop papers. The protocol cards paper (Zaytar, Tadesse, Robinson, Becker-Reshef et al.) models the chain from survey design to cost to label noise to map accuracy, with 17 noise functions on EuroCrops and Zambia. The companion [GeoAI Agency Primitives](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01869) proposes nine primitives for GeoAI assistants (navigation, perception, geo-referenced memory and others) and a benchmark of human productivity in GIS. *Keywords: field surveys, label noise, crop mapping, survey design, GeoAI assistants, ML4RS*

- [Mapping agricultural dynamics in displacement-affected landscapes using satellite foundation model embeddings](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1748-9326/aeabbc): LinkedIn post by Catherine Nakalembe, 1 October 2026: *"Maps and data alone do not equal better policy—it's what we do with them that matters."* The paper (Asare-Ansah, Van Den Hoek, Adebayo, Song & Nakalembe, *Environmental Research Letters* 21, DOI 10.1088/1748-9326/aeabbc, open access) uses 10 m AlphaEarth embeddings to build annual cropland maps, with sample-based area estimates, for northern Uganda's refugee-hosting districts from 2017 to 2024. - [AmirmasoudCS/Crop-Yield-Prediction](https://github.com/AmirmasoudCS/Crop-Yield-Prediction): *Machine learning project investigating whether crop-specific linear regression models can outperform a single global model for crop yield prediction using historical agricultural and environmental data.* A deliberately simple study asking one question: does fitting a separate linear regression per crop beat fitting one model across all crops? On the Kaggle Crop Yield Prediction dataset — 28,242 observations, 101 areas, 10 crop types, 1990–2013, with rainfall, pesticide use and average temperature as features — the crop-specific models reach R² 0.8966 and MAE 15,879.76 against the global baseline's R² 0.6424 and MAE 31,273.75, roughly 49% lower error. The repo walks the full workflow: EDA, preparation, train/validation/test split, global baseline, per-crop models, evaluation, comparison, then coefficient interpretation. Jupyter, MIT. *Keywords: crop yield prediction, linear regression, per-crop models, Kaggle dataset, baseline comparison, coefficient analysis*
  - Worth keeping for the framing rather than the accuracy: it is the cheapest possible version of the specialization argument that runs through [[Foundation_Models]] — does a model per domain beat one model for everything — answered here with linear regression on tabular data instead of pretrained backbones.
  - Treat the numbers with care: yields differ by orders of magnitude between crops, so splitting by crop removes much of the variance a single global model has to absorb, and the gain is partly definitional.

- [Mid-season parcel-based crop type mapping using multi-source time series of big Earth observations and deep learning](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/hazhir-bahrami-ab7723104_github-tenor-inrscropmappingproject-share-7497636823557550080-JnDZ/): LinkedIn publication announcement by lead author Hazhir Bahrami. The framing is that timing is the point, not just accuracy — *"what matters most is having crop-type maps during the growing season (not after it ends) when they can actually inform decisions."* Six models were trained on multi-temporal Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 over Quebec for 2020–2024 — four deep learning (Transformers, hybrid LSTM–Transformers, 1D CNN, LSTM) against Random Forest and XGBoost baselines — mapping corn, soybean, wheat, hay and others, with field boundaries and crop labels from La Financière Agricole du Québec (FADQ) as reference. The Transformer won at 0.97 overall accuracy on validation and 0.93 on test. Two claims the author calls out: applied to entirely unseen 2025 data the models produced mid-season maps at ~92% overall accuracy *"with no additional in-year training data"*, which he positions against *"one of the field's biggest open challenges: uncertainty around future inference"*; and Google's AlphaEarth embeddings fed to plain RF/XGB matched the deep learning models on Sentinel data — with the caveat, stated in the post, that AlphaEarth is an annual product included to gauge embedding quality *"rather than as a direct mid-season equivalent."* Published in *Big Earth Data*, 16 August 2026, Bahrami, Homayouni, Champagne, McNairn, Daneshfar, Chokmani, Nteziyaremye and Zannou, DOI [10.1080/20964471.2026.2705616](https://doi.org/10.1080/20964471.2026.2705616). Code and supporting data: [TENOR-INRS/CropMapping_Project](https://github.com/TENOR-INRS/CropMapping_Project). *Keywords: mid-season crop mapping, Transformer, Sentinel-1/2, AlphaEarth embeddings, unseen year transfer, Quebec FADQ*
  - The annual-vs-mid-season caveat is the interesting tension: an annual embedding product matching per-season deep learning on accuracy does not make it usable for in-season decisions, which is the whole premise of the paper. Related argument on [[Foundation_Models]].
  - Both shortlinks in the post were resolved before filing: the data availability link points at the same GitHub repository as the code link. Tracking parameters were stripped from the post URL.

- [Fields of the World inference app](https://fieldsofthe.world/ftw-inference-app/): Fields of the world inference website. FTW is "an open ecosystem for agricultural field boundary detection" spanning a global dataset, models, inference tooling and web apps, all built on open Sentinel-2. The benchmark holds over 1.6 million labeled parcels across 24 countries on four continents paired with multi-temporal Sentinel-2; the 2024-25 global inference product covers 3.17 billion field polygons across 241 countries at 10 m with confidence scores and F1 between 0.74 and 0.89 across tested countries. The Explorer app browses those boundaries on an interactive map, downloads polygons for a region, and can run inference in the browser with no setup. Sponsored by Taylor Geospatial and co-developed with Microsoft's AI for Good Lab, with ASU's Kerner Lab, WashU and Clark University, hosted via Source Cooperative and Wherobots. *Keywords: FTW, field boundaries, 3.17 billion polygons, Sentinel-2, browser inference, Taylor Geospatial*

The Lacuna Fund, co-founded in 2020 by The Rockefeller Foundation, Google.org and Canada's IDRC, funds equitably labeled datasets for machine learning in low- and middle-income contexts. Examples include image and spectrometry data for five food security crops (cassava, maize, beans, bananas, cocoa) and an open crop-type classification dataset spanning Kenya, Mali, Togo, Rwanda, Uganda, Ethiopia, Malawi, Zambia, Tanzania, Namibia, Sudan and Nigeria. *Keywords: labeled datasets, sub-Saharan Africa, food security crops, crop type classification, open data, ML for development*

- [Côte d'Ivoire Byte-Sized Agriculture Challenge](https://zindi.africa/competitions/cote-divoire-byte-sized-agriculture-challenge/data): Zindi Agricultural Challeneges. A Zindi competition to classify cocoa, oil palm and rubber plantations from multi-temporal Sentinel-2 imagery, using one year of seasonal signal. The twist is that models must work "without access to any geometry-related information, simulating real-world, geometry-free deployment conditions", so features come from imagery alone. Supports Côte d'Ivoire's agricultural sector alongside Tolbi's precision mapping work; €7,000 in prizes, 897 participants. *Keywords: Zindi, Cote d'Ivoire, cocoa palm rubber, Sentinel-2, crop classification, competition*

- [Time2Agri: Temporal Pretext Tasks for Agricultural Monitoring](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04366): Time2Agri: Temporal Pretext Tasks for Agricultural Monitoring. Gupta & Sobti, arXiv 2507.04366, submitted 6 July 2025. Proposes three agriculture-specific self-supervised pretext tasks — Time-Difference Prediction, Temporal Frequency Prediction, and Future-Frame Prediction — that exploit the seasonal cycles of agricultural landscapes rather than generic image pretext tasks. On the SICKLE and FTW India datasets it reports 69.6% IoU for crop mapping and 30.7% MAPE for yield prediction, beating existing remote sensing foundation models. *Keywords: self-supervised learning, pretext tasks, seasonality, yield prediction, SICKLE, crop mapping*

- [From General to Specialized: The Need for Foundational Models in Agriculture](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.05390): From General to Specialized: The Need for Foundational Models in Agriculture. Nedungadi, Xiong, Potze, Van Bree, Lin, Rußwurm & Athanasiadis, arXiv 2507.05390, submitted 7 July 2025. A position paper arguing that although general-purpose foundation models have taken hold in remote sensing and climate science, "their application in challenges related to agriculture...remains under-explored", and that agriculture needs its own model — which they call CropFM — designed for crop mapping, phenology estimation and yield prediction rather than adapted from general ones. *Keywords: CropFM, position paper, domain-specific foundation models, phenology, yield prediction, agriculture AI*

- [agribound](https://github.com/montimaj/agribound): Python package for delineating field boundaries. A toolkit by Sangram Majumdar that combines seven approaches to agricultural field boundary delineation — YOLO object detection, semantic segmentation (FTW, DINOv3, Prithvi), Mask R-CNN instance segmentation, embedding-based clustering, and multi-model ensembling. Works with Landsat, Sentinel-2, HLS, NAIP, SPOT and pre-computed embeddings (AlphaEarth, TESSERA) via Google Earth Engine or local GeoTIFFs, filters non-agricultural polygons using NLCD / Dynamic World / C3S land cover, and exports fiboa-compliant vectors. Apache 2.0. *Keywords: field boundaries, crop mapping, segmentation, Google Earth Engine, fiboa, python package*
  - **Launch post** ([LinkedIn, Sayantan Majumdar, 30 Mar 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sayantanmajumdar_opensource-opensource-activity-7444461276329836544-n2PF)): *"One call. Seven engines. Eight satellite sources. Zero ad hoc scripts."* It added LULC crop filtering, multi-engine ensembling, fiboa-compliant output with evaluation metrics, and 15 example notebooks. The two examples were a supervised DINOv3 fine-tune on NAIP in New Mexico, and a fully unsupervised TESSERA-clustering run in Argentina's Pampas compared against Google Satellite Embeddings. Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.19229666. He says it took about 60 hours with Claude.
  - **Update, v1.0.1** ([LinkedIn, Sayantan "Monty" Majumdar, 30 Sep 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/sayantanmajumdar_remotesensing-gis-agriculture-ugcPost-7511112629743132672-jrS-)): Majumdar presented *"Geospatial AI for Agricultural Field Boundary Delineation: Challenges and Opportunities"* to the NASA EarthRISE/UAH Geo-AI working group. The toolkit now runs one config across Delineate Anything v2, Fields of The World, fine-tuned Mask R-CNN and DINOv3, Prithvi-EO-2.0, label-free clustering of Google Satellite Embedding and TESSERA, and optional SAM 2 refinement. Imagery can be Landsat, Sentinel-2, HLS, NAIP, SPOT 6/7 or your own GeoTIFFs. His finding: *"the barriers we measured to administrative use of AI field boundaries are not the model architecture. They are resolution, reporting practice, and the filters applied after the model."* The SAM 3 backends and the Claude-driven agent are still untested. See [[LinkedIn]].
