- [Satellite Co-location Search Toolkit](https://zenodo.org/records/21982047): Bakhtiari Asl, Dabboor, Tivy & Homayouni, published on Zenodo 17 August 2026, MIT. *"A single-file web application for finding satellite acquisitions that are co-located in space and near-coincident in time over a user-defined area of interest (AOI)"*, aimed at cryosphere and hydrology work where you need several missions over the same target and time window. You upload an AOI as KML, KMZ or GeoJSON, set a date range, and enable any combination of SWOT, ICESat-2, Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-6, Sentinel-3 and GRACE-FO; it queries NASA CMR and the Element84 STAC API, pulls matching granules with footprints, and computes co-locations for every selected pair against a configurable time window and spatial overlap threshold. Output is per-mission counts, co-location tables, an interactive map with per-layer visibility, and a CSV selection manifest exported alongside generated `earthaccess` and `wget` download scripts. Runs entirely in the browser with no server and nothing to install; searching needs no account, and the published file bundles all its libraries so *"it continues to work on restricted networks and over time"*. The limitations are stated plainly: Sentinel-6's 66° inclination misses polar AOIs, Sentinel-3 here means the optical OLCI/SLSTR and SST collections rather than SRAL altimetry (ESA-distributed, absent from NASA CMR), GRACE-FO is a global monthly field matched on time only, and RCM and CryoSat-2 are excluded because their access services cannot be called from a browser. *Keywords: co-location search, multi-mission, NASA CMR, Element84 STAC, cryosphere, single-file web app*
  - The self-contained single-file design is the interesting choice — it trades update-ability for surviving restricted networks and link rot, which is a real constraint for field and polar work.
  - Saeid Homayouni is also a co-author on the Quebec mid-season crop mapping paper on [[Agriculture]].

- [VHRV: Very High-Resolution Benchmark Dataset for Vessel Detection](https://github.com/buyukkanber/vhrv): VHRV: Very High-Resolution Benchmark Dataset for Vessel Detection. 1,502 Google Earth images at 4800×2886 px and 0.1–0.25 m resolution, with 10,158 annotated vessel instances in YOLO horizontal bounding box format. - [M3DRS: Multi-Modal Multi-Resolution Remote Sensing Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/heig-vd-geo/M3DRS): M3DRS: Multi-Modal Multi-Resolution Remote Sensing Dataset. An unlabeled 5-channel dataset (RGB, NIR, nDSM) at 10–25 cm resolution covering roughly 3,077 km² across Switzerland (2,172 km²), France (810 km²) and Italy (95 km²) — about 400,000 images and 491 GB uncompressed. Built by HEIG-VD Geomatic for self-supervised learning research and semantic segmentation, submitted to the NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Track. Aggregates public open data under CC-BY-4.0, with per-source attribution (Swisstopo, Ferrara City, FLAIR #1) in the NOTICE file. *Keywords: multi-modal dataset, self-supervised learning, nDSM, very high resolution, NeurIPS 2025, Switzerland France Italy*

Uses a Scale-MAE framework adapted to 5-band imagery with ViT-Large encoders and UperNet decoders, pretrained on M3DRS and evaluated on FLAIR #1 and STDL-SOILS segmentation benchmarks, with ablations on scratch versus pretrained initialisation and on the value of extra bands.

- [The Technical Debt of Earth Embedding Products](https://cloudnativegeo.org/blog/2026/02/the-technical-debt-of-earth-embedding-products/): Article comparing different earth embeddings. Compares nine Earth observation embedding products (Clay, Major TOM, Earth Index, Copernicus-Embed, Presto, Tessera, AlphaEarth and others) and argues that each ships as a "snowflake" with incompatible formats, tile schemes, and metadata, pushing a large integration burden onto downstream users. Also weighs the cost tradeoff between pixel and patch embeddings, and proposes cloud-native formats, standardized metadata, and open benchmarks as fixes. *Keywords: earth embeddings, GeoParquet, STAC, cloud-native geospatial, vision transformers, interoperability*
