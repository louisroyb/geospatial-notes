- [MaSoN — unsupervised change detection, now on Hugging Face Spaces](https://huggingface.co/spaces/blaz-r/MaSoN-CD-demo): LinkedIn post by Blaž Rolih, 11 September 2026, putting a browser demo online for *Make Some Noise: Unsupervised Remote Sensing Change Detection Using Latent Space Perturbations* (Rolih, Fučka, Wolf, Čehovin Zajc; *IEEE TGRS* 2026, DOI 10.1109/TGRS.2026.3730181). It synthesises "change" as Gaussian noise in latent space, with parameters estimated from the target data, and needs no change masks. It reports +14.1 F1 on average over 5 benchmarks. The demo runs the SSL4EO-trained model zero-shot on two aligned Sentinel-2 RGB images; full performance needs a short label-free fine-tune. *"I'd love to see what you try it on and where it fails"*. Code: [blaz-r/mason_cd](https://github.com/blaz-r/mason_cd), MIT. *Keywords: change detection, unsupervised, latent perturbation, Sentinel-2, Hugging Face, TGRS*
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/blaz-rolih_huggingface-spaces-changedetection-share-7504075843493801984-6GRX); see [[LinkedIn]].

- [Satellite Co-location Search Toolkit](https://zenodo.org/records/21982047): Bakhtiari Asl, Dabboor, Tivy & Homayouni, published on Zenodo 17 August 2026, MIT. *"A single-file web application for finding satellite acquisitions that are co-located in space and near-coincident in time over a user-defined area of interest (AOI)"*, aimed at cryosphere and hydrology work where you need several missions over the same target and time window. You upload an AOI as KML, KMZ or GeoJSON, set a date range, and enable any combination of SWOT, ICESat-2, Sentinel-1, Sentinel-2, Landsat 8/9, Sentinel-6, Sentinel-3 and GRACE-FO; it queries NASA CMR and the Element84 STAC API, pulls matching granules with footprints, and computes co-locations for every selected pair against a configurable time window and spatial overlap threshold. Output is per-mission counts, co-location tables, an interactive map with per-layer visibility, and a CSV selection manifest exported alongside generated `earthaccess` and `wget` download scripts. Runs entirely in the browser with no server and nothing to install; searching needs no account, and the published file bundles all its libraries so *"it continues to work on restricted networks and over time"*. The limitations are stated plainly: Sentinel-6's 66° inclination misses polar AOIs, Sentinel-3 here means the optical OLCI/SLSTR and SST collections rather than SRAL altimetry (ESA-distributed, absent from NASA CMR), GRACE-FO is a global monthly field matched on time only, and RCM and CryoSat-2 are excluded because their access services cannot be called from a browser. *Keywords: co-location search, multi-mission, NASA CMR, Element84 STAC, cryosphere, single-file web app*
  - The self-contained single-file design is the interesting choice — it trades update-ability for surviving restricted networks and link rot, which is a real constraint for field and polar work.
  - Saeid Homayouni is also a co-author on the Quebec mid-season crop mapping paper on [[Agriculture]].

- [Awesome Spectral Indices](https://github.com/awesome-spectral-indices/awesome-spectral-indices): A ready-to-use curated list of Spectral Indices for Remote Sensing applications. Hundreds of indices catalogued by sensing modality (multispectral, hyperspectral, radar, multispectral+thermal) and by domain (vegetation, water, burn, snow, urban, soil, clouds, geology), with NDVI, MNDWI and NBR among them. Each entry carries the formula expression, required bands, constants, classification, sources and contributors under an experimental v1 schema, published as both JSON and CSV. Consumable programmatically through spyndex in Python, an Earth Engine JavaScript module, rgeeExtra in R, SpectralIndices.jl in Julia, and the espectro Streamlit app. MIT. *Keywords: spectral indices, NDVI MNDWI NBR, machine-readable catalogue, spyndex, formula schema, curated list*

- [TerraMesh](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ibm-esa-geospatial/TerraMesh): TerraMesh. A planetary-scale, analysis-ready corpus from IBM and ESA for training geospatial foundation models: seven co-registered sources at 10 m — Sentinel-1 SAR (GRD and RTC), Sentinel-2 (L1C, L2A, RGB), Copernicus DEM, NDVI, and ESA/Impact Observatory/Microsoft land cover — across 9M+ globally distributed samples with multi-year coverage, 85% of radar and optical observations aligned within a week. Built under the ESA-funded FAST-EO project from SSL4EO-S12 and MajorTOM-Core data, CC-BY-SA-4.0. It trained TerraMind-B to a 66.6% average mIoU across six segmentation benchmarks. Cite Blumenstiel et al. 2025, arXiv:2504.11172. *Keywords: TerraMind, pretraining corpus, multimodal co-registered, Sentinel-1 Sentinel-2, IBM ESA, 9 million samples*
  - Related: [[Foundation_Models]], [[Benchmark_Datasets]], [[Data]]

- [UAVScenes: A Multi-Modal Dataset for UAVs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22412): UAVScenes: A Multi-Modal Dataset for UAVs. Wang, Li, Zhang, Yu, Yuan and colleagues, arXiv 2507.22412, submitted 30 July 2025, ICCV 2025. Extends the MARS-LVIG SLAM dataset with manual semantic annotations on both frame-wise camera images and LiDAR point clouds, plus 6-DoF poses and reconstructed 3D maps — around 120,000 labeled pairs across four large scenes (AMtown, AMvalley, HKairport, HKisland). Billed as the first UAV benchmark with semantic labels on both modalities, supporting detection, segmentation, depth estimation, localization, place recognition and novel view synthesis. *Keywords: UAV, LiDAR, multi-modal, SLAM, place recognition, ICCV 2025*

- [VHRV: Very High-Resolution Benchmark Dataset for Vessel Detection](https://github.com/buyukkanber/vhrv): VHRV: Very High-Resolution Benchmark Dataset for Vessel Detection. 1,502 Google Earth images at 4800×2886 px and 0.1–0.25 m resolution, with 10,158 annotated vessel instances in YOLO horizontal bounding box format. - [M3DRS: Multi-Modal Multi-Resolution Remote Sensing Dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/heig-vd-geo/M3DRS): M3DRS: Multi-Modal Multi-Resolution Remote Sensing Dataset. An unlabeled 5-channel dataset (RGB, NIR, nDSM) at 10–25 cm resolution covering roughly 3,077 km² across Switzerland (2,172 km²), France (810 km²) and Italy (95 km²) — about 400,000 images and 491 GB uncompressed. Built by HEIG-VD Geomatic for self-supervised learning research and semantic segmentation, submitted to the NeurIPS 2025 Datasets and Benchmarks Track. Aggregates public open data under CC-BY-4.0, with per-source attribution (Swisstopo, Ferrara City, FLAIR #1) in the NOTICE file. *Keywords: multi-modal dataset, self-supervised learning, nDSM, very high resolution, NeurIPS 2025, Switzerland France Italy*
  - Companion repository: [swiss-territorial-data-lab/proj-vit](https://github.com/swiss-territorial-data-lab/proj-vit) — Vision Transformers for multi-modal remote sensing imagery, a Swiss Territorial Data Lab and HEIG-VD collaboration. Uses a Scale-MAE framework adapted to 5-band imagery with ViT-Large encoders and UperNet decoders, pretrained on M3DRS and evaluated on FLAIR #1 and STDL-SOILS segmentation benchmarks, with ablations on scratch versus pretrained initialisation and on the value of extra bands.

- [1 Introduction | LT-GEE Guide](https://emapr.github.io/LT-GEE/introduction.html): LandTrendr Algorithm on Google Earth Engine. - [The Technical Debt of Earth Embedding Products](https://cloudnativegeo.org/blog/2026/02/the-technical-debt-of-earth-embedding-products/): Article comparing different earth embeddings. Compares nine Earth observation embedding products (Clay, Major TOM, Earth Index, Copernicus-Embed, Presto, Tessera, AlphaEarth and others) and argues that each ships as a "snowflake" with incompatible formats, tile schemes, and metadata, pushing a large integration burden onto downstream users. Also weighs the cost tradeoff between pixel and patch embeddings, and proposes cloud-native formats, standardized metadata, and open benchmarks as fixes. *Keywords: earth embeddings, GeoParquet, STAC, cloud-native geospatial, vision transformers, interoperability*
