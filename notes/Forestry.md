- [PhD position: Multi-temporal forest canopy height reconstruction from satellite data](https://jobs.uzh.ch/job-vacancies/phd-position-multi-temporal-forest-canopy-height-reconstruction-from-satellite-data/f49ee8f9-3652-4f0e-84ab-94c99e374da8): A University of Zurich PhD vacancy, jointly supervised by Prof. Livia Piermattei (Remote Sensing of Environmental Changes, Department of Geography) and Prof. Jan Dirk Wegner (EcoVision Lab, Department of Mathematical Modeling and Machine Learning). It is part of the SNSF project *From Alps to Arctic: Satellite-based Assessment of Forest Canopy Height across Decades*, with collaborators in Switzerland, Austria, Norway, France and Canada. The work: process large archives of optical stereo satellite imagery, develop photogrammetric workflows to produce DSMs and canopy height models, combine these with laser altimetry and machine learning, and build a multi-decade record of forest structure change across alpine and boreal forests. The case for the project is that current large-scale canopy height products are too coarse, cover too short a period, or are weak in remote boreal regions. 80% employment, starting 1 January 2027 or by agreement. *Keywords: canopy height, stereo photogrammetry, boreal forest, Alps, PhD vacancy, University of Zurich*

- [Comparing deforestation data for South American soy](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/data-comparison-deforestation-soy-south-america/): LinkedIn post by Land & Carbon Lab, 23 July 2026, on why three widely used deforestation datasets give different answers for soy supply chains: *"Subtle differences in what each one measures, and how it defines 'forest,' can lead to varying answers."* The linked article by Elise Mazur, Benjamin Wielgosz and Ana Beatriz dos Santos (20 July 2026) compares UMD/Global Forest Watch Tree Cover Loss, MapBiomas land cover transitions and PRODES across Brazil, Argentina and Bolivia. The three disagree because they define forest differently: TCL counts any tree cover over 5 m tall, MapBiomas uses country-specific ecological definitions, and PRODES records human-driven loss of native vegetation regardless of height. Filtering MapBiomas to the FAO forest definition cuts its forest loss by up to 55% in Argentina but barely changes it in Bolivia. Soy-linked deforestation is only 3–12% of total forest loss, depending on country and dataset. The datasets agree on continental hotspots but diverge in regional totals. The advice is to pick the dataset that fits the use, or to compare several independent sources (a "convergence of evidence" approach). The method for aligning local data with AFi, SBTN and FAO definitions is in the WRI working paper [Comparing Forest Loss Data Sources to Align Deforestation- and Conversion-Free Analyses for Soy Production in South America](https://www.wri.org/research/forest-loss-data-sources-deforestation-conversion-free-analyses-soy-production-latin-america) (DOI [10.46830/wriwp.25.00003](https://doi.org/10.46830/wriwp.25.00003)). *Keywords: deforestation data, soy supply chains, forest definitions, PRODES, MapBiomas, Tree Cover Loss*
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/deforestation-soy-supplychains-share-7486066279666089984-qmoq); see [[LinkedIn]]. Both `lnkd.in` links were resolved and their `utm_medium` tracking was stripped.
  - Directly relevant to the EUDR tools on this page, WAC and the Forest Data Partnership models: which forest definition you choose changes what counts as deforestation.

- [WAC — World AgroCommodities](https://github.com/masolele/WAC): *WAC — World AgroCommodities: Earth Observation for Deforestation-Free Supply Chains.* Code, models and utilities from the World AgroCommodities project, which supports the EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) with satellite data. The project aims to map commodity parcels and crop types over demonstration sites, detect deforestation and land conversion after the EUDR's December 2020 cut-off, and give EU Member States open, reproducible methods built on openEO and the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem (CDSE). It also validates the algorithms and supports national uptake. The repository is a Python package (`world_agrocommodities`, managed with `uv`) with optional notebook dependencies. *Keywords: EUDR, deforestation-free supply chains, commodity mapping, openEO, CDSE, land conversion*
  - Pairs with Google's Forest Data Partnership commodity models further down this page: both aim at EUDR compliance, but WAC runs on the European openEO/CDSE stack rather than Earth Engine.

- [NDVI Tree Crown Detection](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/amir-sharifi-313a0160_remotesensing-python-computervision-share-7490188575767154688-zCgy/): LinkedIn post by Amir Sharifi open-sourcing *"a small desktop tool I used couple of years ago for labeling part of my research"* that builds tree crown masks from RGB-NIR imagery. The pipeline is deliberately classical, not learned: load a 4-band (RGB + NIR) TIFF, compute NDVI to find vegetation, clean the mask with morphological operations, then separate touching crowns with a distance transform and watershed segmentation, preview the boundaries, and export a binary PNG mask for use in deep learning datasets. The GUI exposes the NDVI threshold and segmentation parameters, organises processed images, and lets you flag hard cases for manual labelling. He is explicit about scope: *"This isn't intended to replace manual annotation. Instead, it generates a reasonable initial mask that can reduce the amount of time researchers spend labeling tree crowns from scratch."* Repository: [amirsharifi97/NDVI-Tree-Crown-Detection](https://github.com/amirsharifi97/NDVI-Tree-Crown-Detection) — Python, no license declared, last pushed August 2024. *Keywords: tree crown segmentation, NDVI threshold, watershed, pre-annotation, mask generation, RGB-NIR*
  - Pre-annotation rather than detection: it sits upstream of the learned tree crown detectors on this page, such as SelvaBox, by cheapening the labels they need. The same watershed-after-threshold recipe appears in the scikit-image thresholding note on [[Python]].
  - No license is declared on the repository, so it is default copyright — see the licensing watch on [[Code_Repositories]].

- [SelvaBox: A high-resolution dataset for tropical tree crown detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00170): SelvaBox: A high-resolution dataset for tropical tree crown detection. Baudchon, Ouaknine, Weiss, Teng, Walla, Caron-Guay, Pal & Laliberté, arXiv 2507.00170, submitted 30 June 2025 (v2 February 2026). More than 83,000 manually labeled tree crowns in drone imagery at 3–10 cm per pixel across three countries, an order of magnitude larger than earlier tropical forest datasets, aimed at individual tree crown detection that generalises across tropical forest types. *Keywords: tree crown detection, drone imagery, tropical forest, instance annotation, 83000 crowns, benchmark*

- [Forest Data Partnership on Earth Engine](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/datasets/publisher/forestdatapartnership): Forest Data Partnership. Publisher page for datasets supporting "global monitoring of commodity-driven deforestation, forest degradation and restoration efforts". Includes per-pixel commodity probability models for cocoa, coffee, palm and rubber at 10 m (2025a and 2025b versions), and Forest Persistence v0, a 30 m score in [0, 1] for whether a pixel held undisturbed forest in 2020, built by convergence of evidence across sources. Several versions are tagged for EUDR compliance; the datasets are not yet peer-reviewed. *Keywords: deforestation, EUDR, commodity mapping, forest persistence, Google Earth Engine, cocoa palm rubber coffee*
  - Related: [[Google_Earth_Engine]], [[Remote_Sensing]], [[Agriculture]]
