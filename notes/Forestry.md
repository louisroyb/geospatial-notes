- [Could AI unlock finance for nature restoration?](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/ai-model-monitoring-restoration-finance/): Land & Carbon Lab (Stam, Brandt, Rao, Anderson; Aug 2025). DINOv3, calibrated with TerraFund field data, counts trees and measures canopy height from satellite, drone and phone imagery, detecting growth within 8 months of planting: *"Preliminary results are 80% as accurate as traditional forestry methods that measure trees in the field, at 3% of the cost."* It proposes finance structures for agroforestry, ecosystem restoration and restorative value chains. Shared via LinkedIn, with tracking stripped. *Keywords: restoration finance, tree monitoring, DINOv3, TerraFund, MRV, canopy height*

- [Drivers of deforestation alerts — natural or human?](https://globalnaturewatch.org/blog/data-and-tools/drivers-deforestation-alerts): LinkedIn post by Global Forest Watch, 21 October 2025: *"For the first time, we can identify whether alerts of #TreeCoverLoss stem from natural or human causes."* Built with Wageningen University: 11 driver classes at 10 m for the Amazon, Congo Basin and Southeast Asia (2022–2024, monthly updates planned from 2026). A deep learning model on post-disturbance Sentinel-1/2 is trained with Planet imagery, plus VIIRS fire and NBR. - [GEOS-EUDR brief: which global forest maps work for EUDR?](https://zenodo.org/records/17287552): Thünen Institute technical and policy brief No. 1 (Freitas Beyer, Köthke, Lippe; Oct 2025; CC BY 4.0). It reviews 21 global forest and tree cover datasets against the EUDR (cutoff 31 Dec 2020). - [Natural Forests of the World 2020](https://research.google/blog/separating-natural-forests-from-other-tree-cover-with-ai-for-deforestation-free-supply-chains/): Google Research blog post by Maxim Neumann and Charlotte Stanton, 13 November 2025. It introduces a 10 m global map separating natural forest from plantations and other tree cover, as an EUDR 2020 baseline. - [CHMv2 — Meta and WRI's global canopy height map on DINOv3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06382): LinkedIn post by Jessica Ertel (WRI), 11 March 2026, announcing an updated global meter-resolution canopy height map and open model: *"This update includes substantial improvements in accuracy, detail and global consistency with the transition from the DINOv2 -> DINOv3 backbone, diversified training data and improved training parameters."* It is trained on airborne-lidar CHMs and validated against ALS test sets plus tens of millions of GEDI and ICESat-2 shots, and reduces underestimation in tall forests (Brandt, Yi, Tolan, Li, Potapov, Ertel et al.; [arXiv 2603.06382](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06382)). - [Counting individual trees from satellite imagery — TreeMatch and TinyTrees (ECCV 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24786): LinkedIn post by Loic Landrieu, 30 June 2026, on the ECCV 2026 paper *Counting Trees from Satellite Imagery with Noisy Supervision* (Gominski, Mugabowindekwe, Xu, Tong, Brandt, Le, Fensholt, Samaras, Landrieu). The case for counting: *"many applications, such as reforestation monitoring, biomass estimation, and biodiversity assessment, require reasoning at the tree level: detecting and counting individual trees."* The paper makes three contributions. - [Semantic segmentation of forest stands using deep learning](https://www.silvafennica.fi/article/25010): LinkedIn post by SmartForest4.0 (Norway), 26 June 2026, on Sandum, Ørka, Tomic, Næsset & Gobakken, *Silva Fennica* 60(1), article 25010 (DOI 10.14214/sf.25010). - [Comparing deforestation data for South American soy](https://landcarbonlab.org/insights/data-comparison-deforestation-soy-south-america/): LinkedIn post by Land & Carbon Lab, 23 July 2026, on why three widely used deforestation datasets give different answers for soy supply chains: *"Subtle differences in what each one measures, and how it defines 'forest,' can lead to varying answers."* The linked article by Elise Mazur, Benjamin Wielgosz and Ana Beatriz dos Santos (20 July 2026) compares UMD/Global Forest Watch Tree Cover Loss, MapBiomas land cover transitions and PRODES across Brazil, Argentina and Bolivia. The three disagree because they define forest differently: TCL counts any tree cover over 5 m tall, MapBiomas uses country-specific ecological definitions, and PRODES records human-driven loss of native vegetation regardless of height. Filtering MapBiomas to the FAO forest definition cuts its forest loss by up to 55% in Argentina but barely changes it in Bolivia. Soy-linked deforestation is only 3–12% of total forest loss, depending on country and dataset. The datasets agree on continental hotspots but diverge in regional totals. The advice is to pick the dataset that fits the use, or to compare several independent sources (a "convergence of evidence" approach). The method for aligning local data with AFi, SBTN and FAO definitions is in the WRI working paper [Comparing Forest Loss Data Sources to Align Deforestation- and Conversion-Free Analyses for Soy Production in South America](https://www.wri.org/research/forest-loss-data-sources-deforestation-conversion-free-analyses-soy-production-latin-america) (DOI [10.46830/wriwp.25.00003](https://doi.org/10.46830/wriwp.25.00003)). *Keywords: deforestation data, soy supply chains, forest definitions, PRODES, MapBiomas, Tree Cover Loss*
