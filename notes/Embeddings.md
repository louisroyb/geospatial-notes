- [AlphaEarth Foundations Satellite Embeddings on Google Cloud Storage](https://developers.google.com/earth-engine/guides/aef_on_gcs_readme): William Rucklidge's [Google Earth blog post](https://medium.com/google-earth/alphaearth-foundations-satellite-embeddings-now-available-on-google-cloud-storage-f9ab0f7252d6) (1 Dec 2025), shared by [Ollie Guinan](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ollie-guinan-35b13b1_earthobservation-alphaearth-cloudstorage-share-7401433373317230593-QTsb): *"AlphaEarth Foundations Satellite Embeddings are no longer bound strictly to Earth Engine."* The annual dataset is mirrored as COGs in `gs://alphaearth_foundations`:
  - **Layout**: organised by year, then 120 UTM zones, in 8192×8192 tiles with 64 bands (A00–A63), plus index files.
  - **Decoding**: values are int8 (−128 = masked). Divide by 127.5, square, and keep the sign to recover the float.
  - **Cost**: CC-BY 4.0, and *"as of July 2026, the bucket has been updated to 'provider-pays' and users will no longer incur any costs associated with data use or download."*
  - This is the source behind the GeoZarr mosaic and the aef-loader tutorials on this page.
  - *Keywords: AlphaEarth, Google Cloud Storage, COG, int8 quantisation, provider-pays, open access*

- [TESSERA embeddings go open on AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/tessera/): Several posts from 17–30 September 2026 cover the same release, so they are merged into one note. TESSERA is the University of Cambridge's EO foundation model, developed with Clement Atzberger (Arbol). It *"encodes a full year of behavior for every 10-meter pixel on Earth into a compact, 128-dimensional 'embedding'"*, fusing Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 time series. Nine years of global coverage are now free on the AWS Registry of Open Data through the Amazon Sustainability Data Initiative, with no egress fees, and AWS is funding a one-time global pass to finish the historical record. Access is through Cambridge's [geotessera](https://github.com/ucam-eo/geotessera) Python client (MIT, 353 stars) and dClimate's [tessera-embeddings](https://github.com/dClimate/tessera-embeddings) generation code (Apache-2.0). dClimate calls Google's AlphaEarth *"the only comparable effort"* and positions TESSERA as the open alternative. *Keywords: TESSERA, AWS Open Data, Sentinel-1/2, annual embeddings, geotessera, dClimate*
  - **How it works, in 35 minutes**: S. Keshav and D. Coomes' [talk](https://watch.eeg.cl.cam.ac.uk/w/hanDLh2nzjXY4yL1akVEYh) at the Cambridge workshop on foundational AI for ecosystem resilience (24 Nov 2025), recommended by [Atzberger](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/clement-atzberger-8abb8065_tessera-workshop-on-foundational-ai-to-forecast-activity-7402353213728931841-Rt4G): *"within 35 short minutes, you'll also see the working principle of this very smart model."* And an early side-by-side from [Samuel Barrett](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/samuel-barrett-b86b85171_fine-fine-fine-ill-jump-on-board-the-ugcPost-7404302445440421889-8Jj3) (9 Dec 2025): t-SNE of crop pixels shows *"TESSERA forms tighter clusters than AEF, though remember you're looking at a non-linear projection of a 64/128dim embedding space."*
  - **Peer review, home and skills**: TESSERA was [accepted at CVPR 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/activity-7430885989654364160-ZxaU) (reshared by Madeline Lisaius, Feb 2026). - [Planetary Feature Fields are Scalable Earth Representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37784): LinkedIn post by Arjun Rao, 2 October 2026, introducing Planetary Feature Fields (PFFs). This is a planetary-scale hybrid explicit-implicit neural field that represents **14 EO products together**: raw observations, precomputed embeddings and derived maps. It compresses them more than 1800× into a shared, factored 3D feature volume, and a small decoder per product reconstructs each one in its native feature space. *"PFFs are lossy, but retain at least 90% task performance of their source features on high-resolution segmentation and change-detection tasks."* New time periods cost under 3% of the pre-training compute, and products can be added or removed without retraining. Authors: Rao, Loeschcke, Fuller, Corley, Lang, Shelhamer (arXiv 2609.37784). No code link yet. *Keywords: neural fields, Planetary Feature Fields, compression, embedding products, implicit representation, Earth representation*
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/arjunarao_were-introducing-planetary-feature-fields-share-7511798338082770944-vu8j); see [[LinkedIn]].
  - A possible answer to the "too many embedding products" problem raised in Corley's Earth-embeddings taxonomy, further down this page.
  - Related: [[Foundation_Models]], [[Deep_Learning]]

- [MIND the Gap: A Geographic Implicit Neural Representation with Adjustable Spatial Scale](https://research.taylorgeospatial.org/mind/): LinkedIn post by Isaac Corley, 23 September 2026: *"You shouldn't need to switch Earth embedding products just because one task needs broad geographic patterns and another needs local detail."* MIND is a lat/lon location encoder trained Matryoshka-style, so the leading dimensions carry broad geographic patterns and later ones add local detail. - [TerraBit — the whole planet's Clay embeddings, searchable in the browser](https://isaac.earth/terrabit): LinkedIn post by Matt Forrest, 6 July 2026: *"Isaac Corley made the entire planet's Clay embeddings searchable."* Corley binarized the Clay v1.5 Sentinel-2 embeddings, *"which took them from 183GB down to 7GB"*, and stored them as partitioned GeoParquet on S3. DuckDB-WASM then runs a Hamming-distance search over about 5M chips entirely client-side: *"You click anywhere on Earth and it pulls the most similar patches nearby."* There is no server, database or API. The method is written up on GeoSpatial ML as [Compressing Earth Embeddings, pt. 2](https://geospatialml.com/posts/terrabit/), with Caleb Robinson. The stack is TypeScript, Vite, MapLibre GL JS and Web Workers. Code: [isaaccorley/terrabit](https://github.com/isaaccorley/terrabit), Apache-2.0. *Keywords: TerraBit, Clay, binary embeddings, DuckDB-WASM, GeoParquet, similarity search*
  - **The write-up and code** ([LinkedIn, Corley, 7 Apr 2026](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/isaaccorley_what-if-you-could-search-50m-satellite-image-ugcPost-7447124430872522752-qwM5): *"What if you could search 50M satellite image embeddings with no server, no database, and no API?"*). The [GeoSpatial ML post](https://geospatialml.com/posts/terrabit/) explains geohash-partitioned GeoParquet read with HTTP range requests, brute-force Hamming scoring in a Web Worker, and AND/OR/XOR combinations of example tiles. It also tests Google's TurboQuant and finds int4 *"a solid choice"*. [Open-sourced 9 Apr](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/isaaccorley_if-you-enjoyed-the-terrabit-app-1-then-ugcPost-7448064315284283393-hvhH), alongside a `geospatial-frontend` SKILL.md he built *"by scraping my convos with Claude"*. That skill is part of his [geospatial-skills](https://isaac.earth/geospatial-skills) catalogue of 19 agent skills (GDAL, GeoParquet, GeoZarr, TESSERA, PMTiles, STAC, Overture, Source Cooperative), [isaaccorley/geospatial-skills](https://github.com/isaaccorley/geospatial-skills), Apache-2.0.
  - Source: [LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/mbforr_isaac-corley-made-the-entire-planets-clay-share-7479887782547718144-JvNj); see [[LinkedIn]]. The app and repo links come from the post's comments and from web search.

- [Earth Embeddings as Products: Taxonomy, Ecosystem, and Standardized Access](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/isaaccorley_igarss2026-ieeegrss-remotesensing-share-7493330381442535424-pzzo/): LinkedIn post by Isaac Corley presenting the work at IGARSS 2026 in Washington, D.C. *"We take a look at the rapidly growing ecosystem of Earth observation embeddings: how they differ, how they're distributed, and what's needed to make them easier to discover, compare, and use."* The paper argues that geospatial foundation models are fragmenting, that pre-computed embedding products are a practical *"frozen"* alternative to running the models yourself, and that this ecosystem is fragmented in turn — so it proposes a three-layer taxonomy and extends TorchGeo with a unified API so embeddings can be treated as ordinary standardized geospatial datasets. Fang, Stewart, Corley, Zhu & Azizpour, arXiv [2601.13134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13134), submitted 19 January 2026, revised 24 February 2026. Companion book chapter "Earth Embeddings" (Stewart, Fang, Corley & Zhu, arXiv [2608.03410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03410), 4 August 2026) covers embedding types, coverage and resolution characteristics, applications to land cover, ecological and socioeconomic modelling, and guidance on evaluating and publishing embeddings. [Slides](https://isaac.earth/presentation-igarss-2026-earth-embeddings/). *Keywords: Earth embeddings, taxonomy, TorchGeo API, standardized access, IGARSS 2026, embedding products*
  - **The preprint** ([arXiv 2601.13134](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13134); Fang, Stewart, Corley, Zhu, Azizpour), recommended by [Dominik Weckmüller](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/dominik-weckm%C3%BCller_if-youre-into-geospatial-foundation-models-ugcPost-7420398464955596800-flGn) (23 Jan 2026): *"The landscape is quite fragmented at the moment."* He notes hyperspectral data is under-represented, and argues for fusing EO embeddings with OSM, Overture, street-level imagery and VGI, pointing to his own [multilingual geo-textual semantic search paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/s41651-025-00232-5).
